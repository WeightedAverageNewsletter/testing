About Weighted Average

Most newsletters tell you what happened. We tell you what to do with it.

Weighted Average is a family of morning newsletters for Indian professionals who take part in conversations about business but don’t have an hour a day for research reports and financial dailies.

You’ve been in that meeting. Someone mentions FII outflows, a rival’s acquisition, or a company that quietly shut down its AI team, and everyone nods. Weighted Average makes sure you know what it means and have something worth saying back.

How it works

Each edition picks three stories. Not five, not ten. Just the three worth knowing. For each one you get:

What happened. The facts, with the numbers and names that matter.

The second order. What it means one layer deeper, explained the way a sharp colleague would over coffee.

The Weighted Average Question. One line you can use to bring the story up yourself.

The Weighted Average Answer. What to say when someone else brings it up first, written to keep the conversation going.

Every edition closes with In Plain English, a three-sentence explainer of the one concept from that day most likely to trip you up.

The newsletters

Weighted Average Markets: every weekday, just after the Indian market opens
Covers the Nifty and Sensex, FII and DII flows, the rupee, crude, gold, RBI policy and the IPO pipeline. Each edition opens with Today’s Numbers, six indicators you can take in at a glance, followed by the three market stories that matter that day. It’s written for people who don’t work in finance but are expected to have a view on it.

Weighted Average Business: every weekday morning
Covers Indian corporate strategy through real companies. Each edition has one major move from the past week, one pattern showing up across several companies, and one cautionary tale about a strategy that isn’t working. It also includes The Leadership Desk, which covers the week’s most telling leadership change at a Nifty 50 company.

Weighted Average AI in Business: every Friday
Covers how AI is actually changing Indian companies, without the hype. Each edition has the biggest AI move of the week, one company using AI in its day-to-day operations rather than just announcing it, and one AI bet that’s struggling. It also includes The AI Hire Watch, which tracks who is being brought in to run AI at Indian companies. It’s written for people in marketing, sales, HR, operations and strategy, not engineers.

Who it’s for

Mid-career professionals, mostly in their 30s and 40s, with plenty of ambition and very little spare time. You don’t make the deals or write the reports, but you’re part of the conversations about them. And you’d rather not be the person in the room who didn’t know.

A few minutes to read. Useful all day. Free.
